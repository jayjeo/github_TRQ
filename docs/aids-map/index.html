Substitutes and Complements among 40 Products

The cross-price elasticities on this page are obtained by estimating an almost ideal demand system (AIDS) jointly for 40 agricultural products. Each cell shows the percentage change in the quantity demanded of the row product when the price of the column product rises by 1 percent. Outlined cells mark the pairs of products that we judge to be substitutes or complements. Compare this evaluation with the color inside each outline.

Data: April 7, 2022, to March 6, 2025; 153 weekly observations interpolated to 1,065 days Symmetry, homogeneity and adding-up imposed; 858 free demand parameters

Reading the signs

Hicksian elasticity > 0 → substitutes

Consumers buy more of the row product in place of the column product, whose price has risen. For example, when green onion prices rise, consumers buy more scallion.

Hicksian elasticity < 0 → complements

The products are used together, so when the price of one rises, consumers also buy less of the other. For example, when napa cabbage prices rise, consumers also buy less seasoning for winter kimchi.

Own-price elasticity (diagonal) is normally < 0

Consumers buy less of a product when its own price rises. These cells play no part in classifying substitutes and complements, so they are shaded gray; click one to see its value.

Outlines: our evaluation

A solid outline marks a pair that we judge to be substitutes, and a dashed outline a pair that we judge to be complements. Pairs that we judge to be neither (no clear relation, near zero) have no expected sign and are not outlined. Where solid-outlined cells are blue and dashed-outlined cells are amber, the estimated signs agree with our evaluation.

Row i is the product whose quantity demanded responds; column j is the product whose price changes. ● marks the ever-treated products, which had the Quota Tariff in force at least once during the paper's sample period (January 1, 2021, through March 31, 2025). The compensated (Hicksian) elasticities eij and eji always have the same sign (symmetry: wieij = wjeji) and differ in size only by the ratio of the expenditure shares. The uncompensated (Marshallian) elasticity is the Hicksian elasticity plus the income effect (−wjηi). Because each product's expenditure share is small, the Hicksian and Marshallian elasticities usually have the same sign. Substitutes and complements are classified by the Hicksian elasticity (net substitutes and net complements).

      Caveats

      Computation. The elasticities are computed with the Python code in the replication files (AIDS_estimate.py, AIDS_elasticities.py). The estimator is the one that Stata's demandsys aids uses by default: nonlinear least squares (NLS), followed by feasible generalized NLS (FGNLS) iterated to convergence, which is reached after 60 iterations.

      Evaluated pairs. Of the pairs of products that we evaluate, we judge to be substitutes, complements and neither (no clear relation, near zero). The pairs judged to be substitutes or complements are outlined in the heat map.

      Read individual cells with caution. The system estimates 858 demand parameters from 153 weeks of data, and the classification into substitutes and complements is sensitive to how the standard errors are estimated. Look at the overall pattern rather than at individual cells.

      Adjusted standard errors. se_adj = standard error × √(1,065/153) ≈ standard error × 2.64. It is a rough correction only for the dependence between the interpolated daily observations. This map shows only the estimates and their standard errors, not the results of statistical tests. The rule for selecting the products removed from the comparison pool of each ever-treated product, and the resulting lists, are on the page Exclusion lists for the comparison pool.

      Data and processing. The weekly prices and sales revenue of the 40 products are seasonally adjusted product by product; each weekly value is then placed on the Thursday of its ISO week and interpolated to daily values by piecewise cubic Hermite interpolation (PCHIP). Prices are nominal. Spinach prices, whose unit changed in ISO week 21 of 2023, are converted to a per-kilogram basis, and the 2025 price records are moved one week later to match the week definition of the sales data. Sales revenue of young summer radish for ISO weeks 1–7 of 2025 is filled with the values for the same weeks of 2024. For details, see the paper and the replication files.
