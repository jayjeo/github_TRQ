Exclusion Lists for the Comparison Pool

Columns are the prices of the 11 ever-treated products, and rows are the quantities demanded of the 40 products (top: 29 never-treated; bottom: 11 ever-treated). Each cell shows the compensated (Hicksian) cross-price elasticity eij from an almost ideal demand system (AIDS) estimated jointly for the 40 products. The dark cells in each column form the exclusion list of that ever-treated product in the paper's sensitivity analysis for spillovers (blue: substitute list; red: complement list). In comparisons that span a period in which that product has the Quota Tariff in force, observations of the listed products are removed from the comparison pool. A cell in an ever-treated row enters a comparison only when an onset of the column product precedes the first onset of the row product.

Listed cells: q ≤ 0.01 in the estimates from feasible generalized nonlinear least squares (FGNLS) iterated to convergence, and q ≤ 0.10 with the same sign when the nonlinear least squares (NLS) point estimates are combined with panel-corrected standard errors (PCSE).

How to read the grid, and caveats

Direction. Cell (row i, column j) gives the percentage change in the quantity demanded of row product i when the price of column product j rises by 1%. If the Quota Tariff lowers the price of an ever-treated product, the demand for and the price of a substitute (blue) fall with it, so the treated–control difference is compressed and the estimate is biased toward zero; for a complement (red), the bias runs in the opposite direction.

Selection rule. A cell is on a list only if it meets both conditions: a Benjamini–Hochberg q-value of at most 0.01 in the estimates from FGNLS iterated to convergence, and a q-value of at most 0.10, with the same sign, when the NLS point estimates are combined with PCSE, which allow for correlation and differing variances across products. The standard errors are multiplied by √(1,065/153) ≈ 2.64 (se_adj). The multiple-testing correction is applied separately over the 319 cells of the 29 never-treated rows and over the 50 cells in the ever-treated rows that pair two different ever-treated products of the same group (six vegetables, five fruits). Because vegetables and fruits are estimated in separate regressions, the 60 cells that pair an ever-treated vegetable with an ever-treated fruit are not used and have no q-value (shown as “—”). Of the 50 same-group cells, only the 19 in which an onset of the column product precedes the first onset of the row product can enter a comparison. Each list is split by the sign of e into a substitute list (e > 0) and a complement list (e < 0).

How the lists are applied. For each ever-treated product, only the products on the list in its column are removed. A control observation of a product on comparison date t is dropped from the horizon-h regression if an ever-treated product whose list contains that product has the Quota Tariff in force on any day of [t−120, t+h]. When the Quota Tariff is in force for several ever-treated products within that window, their lists are combined. The paper reports the estimates with substitutes and complements removed, with only substitutes removed and with only complements removed (the buttons above).

The three closely related products. Young napa cabbage, scallion and young summer radish are closely related to napa cabbage, green onion and radish, respectively. Because of the limitations below, they cannot be screened out by the estimated elasticities alone, so they are dropped from every vegetable comparison in all three exclusion specifications, whatever their estimated elasticities. In the grid, the cell of each pair is outlined, and a note appears below the row name. Of the three, only scallion ← green onion is also on a list on the basis of its estimated elasticity.

Limitations. The demand system treats prices as exogenous and estimates 858 demand parameters from 153 weeks of data on the 40 products. Over most of the estimation period, at least one ever-treated product had the Quota Tariff in force, so the estimated elasticities may also reflect the supply shocks that prompted designations. The classification into substitutes and complements is sensitive to how the standard errors are estimated. The exclusion lists therefore contain candidates selected by the estimated elasticities, and they do not establish which products are substitutes or complements. Soybean sprout has no row because the point-of-sale data used for the demand system do not cover it.
